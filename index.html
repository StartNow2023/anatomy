Anatomy NotesLearning human anatomy, step by step

Following the order of a systematic anatomy textbook: first the language, then the skeleton, then muscles, organs and nerves — one layer at a time. Each stage has its own page and ends with a short quiz.

Eight stages

The order is Terms → Bones → Joints → Muscles → Internal organs → Heart & vessels → Sense organs → Nervous system. The organs come before the nerves and vessels because nerves and vessels run all over the body to supply the organs — once you know the organs, it is much easier to see what each nerve and vessel is for. The endocrine system is usually covered briefly at the end.

You don't have to finish one layer before peeking at the next. While learning a muscle you'll meet the nerve that supplies it, and that's fine. Your ticks are saved only in your own browser.

How to study

✋ Feel your own body

Bony landmarks and working muscles can all be found on yourself. Once you've felt something, you remember it far better than after reading about it ten times.

✏️ Draw it

After studying a diagram, close the book and draw it from memory, then compare. Whatever you can't draw is what you haven't understood yet.

🧊 Use 3D models

Anatomy is three-dimensional, and flat pictures make it hard to see how things fit together. A free 3D anatomy app you can rotate helps a lot.

🔁 Review every few days

Use a flashcard app such as Anki, or come back every day or two and take the quiz again.

Free resources you can use

Most anatomy pictures online are copyrighted and can't simply be copied onto your own website. These are openly licensed and free to learn from:

OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology

A free university textbook under a CC BY 4.0 licence — you can reuse its figures and text as long as you credit it.

Gray's Anatomy (1918) plates

The classic illustrations, now in the public domain. Every image on Wikimedia Commons lists its licence — check it before you use one.

Servier Medical Art

A library of medical illustrations under CC BY 4.0, handy for your own study notes.

Z-Anatomy

An open-source 3D anatomy atlas you can download for free and rotate to look at bones and muscles.
