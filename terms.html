Stage 0 · Anatomical TermsThe language of anatomy

Directional terms are the language of anatomy. Learn them first, and whenever a book or a diagram says "superior" or "lateral", you'll know exactly where it means.

Step 1: the anatomical position

Every directional term is based on this standard position:

  • Standing upright, looking straight ahead
  • Arms hanging at the sides, palms facing forward
  • Feet together, toes pointing forward

Whether the person is actually lying down, face down or moving, always imagine them in this position when you describe where something is.

Left and right always mean the left and right of the person being described. When they face you, their right hand is on your left.

The three planes

Imagine slicing the body with a knife: each direction of the cut is a different plane. When you look at a CT or MRI scan, or a cross-section in a book, first work out which plane you're looking at.

Organs can be cut too: a cut along an organ's long axis is a longitudinal section, and a cut straight across it is a cross section.
