Stage 1 · BonesThe skeleton

An adult has about 206 bones, but there's no need to memorise them one by one. First get to know the major bones, then find the bony landmarks on your own body. The skeleton is the map you'll use later for joints and muscles.

Total206

206 is the usual number for adults. A newborn has around 270–300 bones, and many of them fuse as the child grows. Some people have an extra bone or one fewer (an extra cervical rib or extra sesamoid bones, for example), and that's normal too. The stapes in the ear is about the size of a grain of rice — the smallest bone in the body.

Four shapes of bone

📏 Long bones

Tube-shaped with wider ends, mostly in the limbs, where they work like levers. Examples: humerus, femur, phalanges.

🧊 Short bones

Small and roughly cube-shaped, often in groups; they take pressure and allow small movements. Examples: carpals, tarsals.

🛡️ Flat bones

Thin plates that often form a wall around organs to protect them. Examples: the bones of the skull roof, sternum, ribs, scapula.

🧩 Irregular bones

Bones with complicated shapes that don't fit the other groups. Examples: vertebrae, and the temporal and sphenoid bones of the skull.

There is also a special kind, the sesamoid bone, which grows inside a tendon and works like a pulley for the muscle. The biggest one is the patella (kneecap).

Find them on your body

Bony landmarks are bumps of bone you can feel through the skin. Tick each one when you find it — ticks are saved only in your own browser — and tap Show me to see where it is on the skeleton. Goal: find a dozen or more and you've passed this stage.
